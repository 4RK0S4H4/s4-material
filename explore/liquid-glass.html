Today’s S4 Orion glass is a blur with a tint. This lab tries a liquid glass, close to Apple’s Liquid Glass: each glass shape behaves like a thick slab whose edges curve down, so what’s behind it bends near the rim (light refracts as it would through real glass), with a light blur, a specular highlight on the edge and a soft shadow. Nearby glass shapes can melt into each other like drops.

Try it below: scroll the app so photos and text slide under the bars, open the menu (top right), press and slide along the nav bar, and drag the shapes in the playground. Switch theme and color at the top. Then pick one option per question by tapping its card (not the preview); answers are saved with your other labs.

How It Would Ship in S4 Orion

  • Same places as today. Liquid glass replaces the material of the surfaces decision 0004 already makes glass (nav bar, toolbars, top app bar when scrolled, menus, sheets, dialogs). Content stays solid.
  • Same guarantee. Text on glass stays on-surface at WCAG AA. “Fixed Tint” reuses today’s proof unchanged; “Adaptive Tint” checks every pixel and raises the tint only where needed, so the floor holds by construction.
  • Chrome and Edge: real refraction over any page through an SVG filter on backdrop-filter (the “SVG” engine here). Shapes don’t melt in this engine.
  • Safari (iPhone, Mac) and Firefox: they don’t support SVG filters in backdrop-filter yet (WebKit has a fix in review). Until then, liquid glass needs WebGL, which only works over things the app draws itself (photos, maps, charts, canvas). Everywhere else they get today’s glass plus the new specular rim and shadow, so the look stays close.
  • Fallbacks unchanged: high contrast, reduced transparency and data-glass="off" make every glass surface solid; reduced motion keeps melting and lens effects off.
